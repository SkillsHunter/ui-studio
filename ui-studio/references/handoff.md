# Handing it over to a non-technical owner

Keep the hand-off short and in their words. Include only what applies.

## Replace before publishing
List first, in order of importance: where orders or messages go (email, phone), then each placeholder: what belongs there, ideal size or shape ("a landscape photo of your counter, at least 1600px wide").

## Keeping it up to date
- Point to the content block at the top of the file and show one real edit from this page (change an opening time, add a menu item, mark a game cancelled).
- Say what to do if they break something: keep the original file as a backup copy and go back to it.
- If updates will be frequent and they're not comfortable editing a file, offer the simplest alternative that fits (their website builder's editor, or reading the schedule from a Google Sheet they already use) and what it would take.

## Publishing
Pick one route that fits them and explain it in three or four steps:
- **Free and fastest:** Netlify Drop (drag the file onto app.netlify.com/drop), then optionally connect a domain they own.
- **They already have Squarespace, Wix or WordPress:** paste into a custom-code or HTML block, or rebuild the sections with these colours and fonts.
- **Shared Claude artifact:** fine for previews and simple sharing; links and email buttons may be limited inside the preview.
Name any account they must create and any yearly cost (a domain is usually $10–20 a year). Never say a page is live unless it is.

## Being found
- The page has a clear title, description and share preview.
- Local businesses: claim or update a free Google Business Profile with the same name, address, hours and site link, and keep them identical everywhere.
- No promises about search rankings.

## Privacy and accessibility
- Say what personal information the page collects and where it goes (e.g. "orders arrive in your email inbox; nothing is stored on the website").
- The page follows common accessibility practice (contrast, keyboard use, labels, alt text), but automated checks aren't a legal certification. Name anything you couldn't test.
