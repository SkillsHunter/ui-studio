# What makes a page look machine-made

This list catches defaults; it is not a style to aim for. Avoiding everything here can still produce a recognisable "anti-AI" look, so choose from the subject, not from this page.

These are the defaults language models fall into. Each is fine when the person asks for it. When they didn't, using one means you didn't decide; replace it with a choice made for this subject. The checker flags the starred (*) ones automatically.

## Structure
- The template page: hero, a row of three icon-plus-heading cards, testimonials, a sign-up band, a four-column footer, regardless of subject.
- Content chopped into identical rounded cards with the same shadow. A card inside a card, ever.
- The "hero metric": a giant number with a small label and stat row, unless the number is real and is the point.
- Numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03) on things that aren't a real sequence.
- Every section separated the same way with the same gap.
- An email sign-up as the closing section of a page whose job isn't a mailing list.
- A fake product screenshot built from grey boxes, or a hand-drawn browser window with traffic-light dots.*

## Type
- * A small, letter-spaced, ALL-CAPS label sitting above a heading (the "eyebrow"). Delete it; let the heading speak.
- * One word in a headline set apart in italic, a different colour, a highlight or gradient.
- * Gradient text.
- Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Poppins, Montserrat, Lato or the system font as the voice of the page. (Fine for app UI body text when familiarity matters.)
- Monospace used as a costume for "techy", rather than for code or numbers.
- More than two typefaces.

## Colour and surface
- * Purple-to-blue or pink-to-purple gradients; glowing blurred blobs or orbs behind text.
- The cream background + big serif + terracotta accent look (near #F4F1EA / #D97757), and the near-black page with one acid-green accent, when the subject didn't ask for them.
- The "premium hospitality" look: espresso or chocolate brown with cream, a fashionable soft serif (Young Serif, Fraunces, Recoleta, Instrument Serif), rounded cards stacked beside the headline. Tasteful, and anonymous.
- The admin-dashboard card kit on a subject that isn't software: a navy or slate header bar, white rounded cards in a column, pill filters, green "On" badges. Correct, and it reads as a back-office portal, not a club, shop or practice.
- The quiet "editorial minimal" look: pale paper, hairline rules, a narrow centred column, almost no colour. Reads as a design exercise, not a business.
- * Pure #000 and #fff everywhere; grey text on coloured backgrounds.
- A framework's stock palette (Tailwind indigo/violet/slate) instead of colours mixed for the subject.
- Glass/blur panels as decoration. Soft identical shadows under everything.
- Hard offset "sticker" shadows and thick black outlines on everything (neo-brutalist costume), unless the subject really is that loud.

## Chrome and copy
- * Template punctuation: "A · B · C" meta strings, arrows glued to button text ("Learn more →"), little coloured status dots as decoration.
- * Emoji used as icons.
- * Buzzwords: seamless, robust, empower, supercharge, elevate, effortless, unlock, unleash, revolutionize, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, game-changer, synergy, leverage (as a verb), "in today's fast-paced world".
- * Placeholder people and companies (Jane Doe, John Smith, Acme, Lorem ipsum) left in the page.
- Invented testimonials, ratings, client logos or "Trusted by 10,000+ teams".
- Decorative text with nothing to say: "Scroll" cues, rotated vertical labels, version badges in the hero.

## Motion
- The same fade-and-slide-up on every section, hover-lift on every card.
- Bouncy easing on ordinary interface changes.
- Anything that animates layout (width, height, top, left) or uses `transition: all`.*
